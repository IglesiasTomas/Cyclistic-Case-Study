# Cyclistic Bike-Share: Understanding Customers to Drive Membership Growth

## Project Overview

This project analyzes 12 months of Cyclistic bike-share trip data to identify
behavioral differences between annual members and casual riders.

The objective is to understand how casual riders use the service and identify
opportunities to convert them into annual members.

This project was completed as the capstone project for the
Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate.

---

## Business Questions

The analysis aims to answer three main questions:

- How do annual members and casual riders differ?
- Why might casual riders buy Cyclistic annual memberships?
- How can Cyclistic use digital media to influence casual riders to become members?

---

## Dataset

The analysis uses Cyclistic trip data covering the period:

**August 2025 – July 2026**

After combining the available datasets and removing incomplete,
inconsistent, negative, and sub-60-second rides, approximately:

**3.9 million valid rides**

remained for analysis.

---

## Analysis

The analysis focuses on several dimensions of rider behavior:

### Rider Type
Comparison of the proportion of rides made by members and casual riders.

### Ride Duration
Comparison of average ride duration between the two rider types.

### Seasonality
Analysis of how usage changes throughout the year and how seasonal
patterns differ between members and casual riders.

### Day of Week
Comparison of weekday and weekend usage.

### Time of Day
Analysis of hourly usage patterns and differences between rider types.

### Geographic Distribution
Comparison of the most frequently used stations by members and casual riders.

---

## Key Findings

- Casual riders account for approximately 36% of rides.
- Casual riders have significantly longer rides than members.
- Casual rider activity is concentrated during the summer.
- Casual riders are more active on weekends.
- Members show stronger usage during weekdays and commuting hours.
- Casual riders are concentrated around lakefront and public park areas.

Overall, the data suggests that members use Cyclistic more consistently
as transportation, while casual riders display behavior more consistent
with leisure-oriented trips.

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Targeted Marketing Campaign

Launch campaigns targeting casual riders during April and May, before the
high season.

Campaigns could receive additional focus during weekends and around
lakefront and public park areas, where casual riders are highly concentrated.

### 2. Collect Additional Customer Data

Combining trip data with customer-level information such as age range,
number of trips, and approximate location could allow Cyclistic to create
more precise customer segments and marketing campaigns.

### 3. Explore Seasonal or Weekend Membership Options

Cyclistic could investigate summer-exclusive or weekend-exclusive
membership options at a lower price point.

This should only be implemented after analyzing customer behavior,
pricing and financial viability.
